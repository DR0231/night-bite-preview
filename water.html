Structure key

First-person notebook: how I read the lake for cats and low-light predators.

Reference notes from the ODNR fishing map and public fishing write-ups, not first-hand nights yet.

  • Old Big Walnut channel still runs under the lake. I treat it as the buried highway, where bait and current often line up after dark.
  • Dam / main basin is the deepest water on the 894 map (50 to 60 ft on that chart). Big water, boat traffic by day, more room to dig at night.
  • Causeway / Smothers cuts are the pinch points: bridges, cuts, and current funnels where I listen for bait and watch the graph harder.
  • Walnut Road lower ramp: nearby flat vs drop. I use the flats for bank work and the nearby break when I’m in a boat and want a quick edge.
  • Oxbow / upper arms: shallower, more bank and night work. Quieter when the middle lake is busy; watch for silted edges.
  • 10 hp and 10 mph limits apply, with idle speed within 100 ft of shore. Hoover is a Columbus drinking-water reservoir: keep it clean, keep wakes down, follow posted limits.
  • Old Alum Creek channel still runs under the lake. Its ledges, and the submerged roads, hold saugeye and cats.
  • Dam riprap and the causeways (the road crossings) are the rock: saugeye at dusk and after dark, muskie trolling in summer.
  • Coves north of the Cheshire Road bridge are the spring crappie and bass water, and they are no wake.
  • South main lake is unlimited horsepower. Expect wakes and big boats by day; it calms after dark.
  • Drinking-water reservoir. Keep it clean and follow the Corps and state park rules.
  • Shallow everywhere. ODNR draws this lake in 2 ft contours because it is so flat. Stumps and shallow flats are part of every trip.
  • Riprap and the old towpath drop-offs are the hard bottom saugeye relate to.
  • Docks, pilings and stumps hold crappie and bluegill. Fish them from public shore or a boat, never from a private dock.
  • Flats near the deeper holes are where channel cats feed after dark.
  • No wake within 300 ft of any land mass. Idle in the dark and watch for stumps.
  • Deep end at the dam. ODNR puts the deepest water, over 40 ft, near the dam; much of the rest is 20 ft or less.
  • Rock south, mud north. ODNR says the bottom is rock in the south section and mud and silt in the north.
  • A dammed Scioto River. ODNR’s 10 ft contour map shows where the deeper water runs; open it before a night trip.
  • Hybrid striped bass are stocked here (ODNR Fishing Tips). Watch for schools pushing shad to the top in summer.
  • 40 mph, idle within 100 ft of shore and bridges. A Columbus drinking-water reservoir: keep it clean.
  • Long and narrow. About six miles of river reservoir; ODNR says most of it is 10 to 15 ft deep, over 20 ft near the dam.
  • Rain changes everything. ODNR notes the lake is generally clear but turns muddy after heavy rain.
  • Hybrid striped bass chase shad near the surface in summer; ODNR says diving gulls give the schools away.
  • Fishing access on the west side south of Fishinger Road, and along the east side city park (ODNR).
  • 40 mph, idle within 100 ft of shore and bridges. A Columbus drinking-water reservoir: keep it clean.
  • Shallow and productive. ODNR draws this lake in 2 ft contours. Channels, islands and weed edges are the structure.
  • Saugeye grow fast here (ODNR): one of Ohio’s best saugeye lakes by angler reports and gill nets.
  • Old Field Beach jetties are the big shore spots; ODNR lists large fishing jetties there.
  • South Fork of the Great Miami River near Moundwood gets a spring white bass run (ODNR).
  • No wake within 300 ft of any shoreline and in the channels. Idle in the dark.
  • Crappie lake. ODNR says both black and white crappie reproduce well, with many over nine inches. Brush and wood hold them.
  • A dammed Olentangy River. ODNR’s 3 ft contour map shows where the deeper water runs; open it before a night trip.
  • Lake level moves. A Corps flood control lake: high after rain, about 5 ft down in a winter drawdown.
  • Channel cats are thick here (ODNR hoop nets: three times the statewide rate), mostly under 15 inches.
  • Unlimited horsepower. Expect boats by day; it calms after dark.

Lake rules

Boating rules from the agency that runs Hoover, with the source and the date checked. Rules change, so check the official page before you launch.

Boating on Hoover 10 hp, 10 mph
  • Boats up to 10 hp, 8 to 22 ft long (sailboats up to 25 ft). Rec and Parks lists the max as 9.9 hp.
  • No motorized boat over 10 mph, and idle speed within 100 ft of shore.
  • A motor over 10 hp has to stay tilted up or have its prop removed.
  • No water skiing and no personal watercraft. Rec and Parks also lists no wakeboarding or tubing.
  • No boating within 100 ft downriver of the dam.
  • No swimming in any City of Columbus reservoir.

Sources: Columbus City Code 921.01; Columbus Rec and Parks, Hoover Reservoir. Questions: Columbus Watershed Management, 614-645-1721. Checked Oct. 27, 2026.

Boating on Alum Creek no hp limit, no wake zones
  • ODNR: outboard motors of unlimited horsepower are allowed, but portions of the lake are no wake zones. Follow the buoys.

Source: ODNR, Alum Creek Lake Fishing Area. State park: ODNR, Alum Creek State Park. Checked Oct. 27, 2026.

Boating on Buckeye Lake no hp limit, 300 ft no wake
  • ODNR: no horsepower restrictions.
  • ODNR’s fishing map marks a 300 ft no wake zone along the shore.

Sources: ODNR, Buckeye Lake Fishing Area; ODNR fishing map. State park: ODNR, Buckeye Lake State Park. Checked Oct. 27, 2026.

Boating on O’Shaughnessy no hp limit, 40 mph
  • Columbus Rec and Parks: unlimited horsepower, 40 mph maximum speed.
  • Maximum length 22 ft for all vessels.
  • No jet skis, tubing or swimming in any City of Columbus reservoir.
  • Idle speed within 100 ft of the shoreline or bridges on all City reservoirs.
  • Park hours are 7 a.m. to 11 p.m.

Sources: Columbus Rec and Parks, O’Shaughnessy Reservoir; Land Stewardship Columbus, FAQs (idle rule); Columbus City Code 921. Questions: Columbus Watershed Management, 614-645-1721. Checked Oct. 29, 2026.

Boating on Griggs no hp limit, 40 mph
  • Columbus Rec and Parks: unlimited horsepower, 40 mph maximum speed.
  • Maximum length 22 ft for all vessels.
  • No jet skis, tubing or swimming in any City of Columbus reservoir.
  • Idle speed within 100 ft of the shoreline or bridges on all City reservoirs.
  • Park hours are 7 a.m. to 11 p.m.
  • The Trabue-Murphy ramp is closed until further notice.

Sources: Columbus Rec and Parks, Griggs Park; Land Stewardship Columbus, FAQs (idle rule); Columbus City Code 921. Questions: Columbus Watershed Management, 614-645-1721. Checked Oct. 29, 2026.

Boating on Indian Lake no hp limit, 300 ft no wake
  • ODNR: no horsepower limits.
  • ODNR’s fishing map: open to speed except within 300 ft of any shoreline, in channels, or where marked no wake. Speed zones are marked with “open zone” buoys.
  • Park hours are 6 a.m. to 11 p.m.; ODNR allows legitimate recreation, like fishing, outside those hours.

Sources: ODNR, Indian Lake Fishing Area; ODNR fishing map. State park: ODNR, Indian Lake State Park. Checked Oct. 29, 2026.

Boating on Delaware Lake no hp limit
  • ODNR: boating with unlimited horsepower on the reservoir, with three boat ramps.
  • Follow posted no wake buoys and swim area markers.
  • Park hours are sunrise to sunset; ODNR allows legitimate recreation, like fishing, outside those hours.

Source: ODNR, Delaware State Park. Lake: ODNR, Delaware Lake Fishing Area. Checked Oct. 29, 2026.
