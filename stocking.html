ODNR’s Buckeye Lake fishing area page has no stocking tables, net surveys, angler survey or fishing forecast. This page says so rather than guess.

What ODNR does say: Buckeye Lake was built about 1830. It averages about 6 ft deep with a maximum of about 14 ft, and has a relatively flat bottom without much underwater woody cover. No horsepower limit. Source: ODNR, Buckeye Lake Fishing Area.

ODNR’s O’Shaughnessy Reservoir fishing area page has no stocking tables, net surveys, angler survey or fishing forecast. This page says so rather than guess.

What ODNR does say: the City of Columbus built the reservoir in 1925 on the Scioto River. It is 912 acres with 18.4 miles of shoreline, over 40 ft deep near the dam though much of it is 20 ft or less, with rock bottom in the south and mud and silt in the north. Source: ODNR, O’Shaughnessy Reservoir Fishing Area. ODNR’s Hybrid Striped Bass Fishing Tips list O’Shaughnessy among the lakes the Division of Wildlife stocks with hybrid striped bass; the numbers aren’t posted there.

ODNR’s Griggs Reservoir fishing area page has no stocking tables, net surveys, angler survey or fishing forecast. This page says so rather than guess.

What ODNR does say: the dam was built on the Scioto River in 1904, making a narrow lake about six miles long with 387 surface acres. Depths can pass 20 ft near the dam, but most of the lake is 10 to 15 ft. It is generally clear but turns muddy after heavy rain. Source: ODNR, Griggs Reservoir Fishing Area. ODNR’s Hybrid Striped Bass Fishing Tips list Griggs among the lakes stocked with hybrid striped bass, and ODNR’s June 12, 2026 news release says Griggs has a strong population of large hybrids, often over 20 inches. No numbers are posted.

ODNR’s lake PDFs run through 2019. Newer stocking is in ODNR’s Fish Stocking Database on DataOhio, which asked for a sign in, so it isn’t copied here. Checked Oct. 27, 2026.

ODNR’s fishing forecast

  • Blue catfish were first stocked in 2011, and some of the older fish already weigh over thirty pounds.
  • Saugeye have rebounded: average to above average stocking success since 2017, with many sizes from 10 to 20 inches.
  • Walleye were stocked in 2019 for the first time in over thirty years, part of a three year walleye and saugeye study.
  • Crappie trap nets show an even split of fish above and below the nine inch minimum length.
  • Most bass sampled are 12 to 18 inches. White bass run up Big Walnut Creek in spring.

Summarized from the fishing forecast on ODNR, Hoover Reservoir Fishing Area. ODNR doesn’t date the forecast.

  • A strong 2016 year class of largemouth bass should give good numbers of 13 to 16 inch fish.
  • Some of the best smallmouth fishing in the state, many 10 to 14 inches and some reaching 5 to 6 pounds.
  • Saugeye stocked in 2017 survived very well, giving many fish of 17 to 20 inches.
  • Recent crappie trap nets had the best catch rate of crappie over 11 inches in ten years.
  • Muskie are stocked every year: usually best May to July in the lake and March to April in the spillway.

Summarized from the fishing forecast on ODNR, Alum Creek Lake Fishing Area. ODNR doesn’t date the forecast.

  • One of Ohio’s best saugeye lakes by angler reports and gill nets: stocked fish survive well and grow fast in this shallow, productive lake.
  • Crappie have done very well, with many black and white crappie over nine inches and a real chance at 14 to 16 inch fish.
  • Largemouth bass do well in the shallow water and weeds; good numbers, though most are under 15 inches.
  • One of the best bluegill lakes in central Ohio, where fish over eight inches are caught consistently.
  • Flathead catfish have grown in number over the past decade, with fish of 20 to 30 pounds caught every year. White bass run up the South Fork of the Great Miami River near Moundwood in spring.

Summarized from the fishing forecast on ODNR, Indian Lake Fishing Area. ODNR doesn’t date the forecast.

  • Best known for crappie: both black and white crappie reproduce well, and trap nets show many fish over nine inches.
  • Crappie catch rates are above average, and crappie tournament fish average about one pound.
  • Largemouth bass numbers have trended down over five years, but there are still good numbers, mostly 12 to 15 inches.
  • Channel catfish hoop nets caught three times the statewide average rate, the highest in central Ohio, though about three quarters were under 15 inches.
  • Bluegill and white bass can be caught in good numbers at different times of the year.

Summarized from the fishing forecast on ODNR, Delaware Lake Fishing Area. ODNR doesn’t date the forecast.
