Species

Hoover hub for all listed species: cats, bass, panfish, saugeye/walleye, and a few incidentals. Alum Creek hub: saugeye, muskie, smallmouth and largemouth, crappie and the rest of the lake's listed species. Buckeye Lake hub: saugeye, crappie, panfish and channel cats in a shallow, fast warming lake. Night is especially useful for channel and flathead (blues too); it is not the best approach for every species. Night is especially useful for saugeye and channel cats; muskie and crappie are mostly low light and daytime fish. What’s biting now

at Hoover

    Changes each month. Typical patterns from the species pages and ODNR, not a fishing report.

    Channel catfish

    Hoover’s workhorse night fish, reliable on cut bait and crawlers from the bank or a 10 hp boat.

    Present at Alum Creek. Night flats and creek channel edges with cut bait or crawlers; not the headline fish here.

    Abundant at Buckeye. Shallow flats near the deeper holes after dark, cut bait or crawlers.

    Blue catfish

    Trophy fishery. Blues were first stocked in 2011 and have become a Hoover highlight.

    Flathead catfish

    Strong flathead presence. Live or fresh bait near wood, rock, and channel structure after dark.

    Bullhead

    Listed by ODNR at Buckeye. Small, easy night cats on crawlers from the bank; no Ohio daily limit listed.

    Largemouth bass

    More abundant than smallmouth on Hoover. Docks, weeds, and shoreline cover (public water).

    Cove cover and shoreline wood, best in the no wake coves north of Cheshire Road.

    Docks, weeds and shoreline cover in a shallow, fast warming lake.

    Smallmouth bass

    Present but less abundant than largemouth. Look to rock, points, and clearer main-lake edges.

    A real Alum Creek target. Rock, points and clearer main lake edges, moving up early in spring.

    Black crappie

    Solid Hoover panfish: spring banks and brush, then deeper wood and channel edges.

    Coves north of Cheshire Road in spring, then brush, docks and standing wood.

    Buckeye warms first in spring. Shallow docks, stumps and brush, bobbers and minnows.

    White crappie

    Shares Hoover with black crappie, with the same spring bank push and deeper summer brush.

    Shares the coves and wood with black crappie, with the same spring push.

    Same shallow cover as black crappie; spring fish in under 2 ft of water.

    Bluegill / sunfish

    Good bluegill fishing at Hoover: easy bank sessions, and bait for the cooler where legal.

    Easy bank fishing in the coves and around docks at the beach and marina areas (public access only).

    Docks, weeds and shallow bays. Easy summer bank sessions from public shore.

    Yellow perch

    Listed by ODNR at Buckeye. Rock and weed edges, minnows or crawlers near the bottom; less steady than crappie.

    White bass

    Confirmed on the ODNR Hoover page, with a spring run up Big Walnut Creek and summer schools chasing shad.

    Listed by ODNR for Alum Creek. Schools chasing shad in summer and fall.

    Hybrid striped bass (wiper)

    Possible in many Ohio reservoirs and share the striped/white combined limit. White bass are confirmed at Hoover; treat hybrids as “possible / verify reports,” not a guarantee.

    Saugeye

    Stocked saugeye have rebounded since about 2017, a real Hoover target for low-light trolling and casting.

    A main Alum Creek target. Dam riprap, causeway rock and channel ledges, best at dusk and after dark.

    Buckeye's signature fish, stocked by ODNR. Riprap, hard bottom and old towpath drop-offs at dusk and night.

    Walleye

    Stocked in 2019 as part of a study against saugeye. They’re here through stocking, so don’t assume a huge wild population.

    Listed for Alum Creek alongside saugeye; they share the combined walleye/saugeye/sauger limit.

    Muskellunge

    Stocked by ODNR at Alum Creek. A fish of 10,000 casts, best in fall on big baits along weed edges and causeways.

    Common carp

    Incidental and intentional carp fishing on public banks. A good fight, and not on the statewide limits table.

    Longnose gar

    Incidental oddball, occasionally encountered and not a primary Night Bite target.

    Limits on species pages are reminders only. Confirm on HuntFish OH / ODNR Fishing Regulations 2026–27 (verify current).
