Species

Hoover hub for all listed species: cats, bass, panfish, saugeye/walleye, and a few incidentals. Alum Creek hub: saugeye, muskie, smallmouth and largemouth, crappie and the rest of the lake's listed species. Buckeye Lake hub: saugeye, crappie, panfish and channel cats in a shallow, fast warming lake. O’Shaughnessy hub: stocked hybrid striped bass, saugeye and blue cats, plus crappie, bass, bluegill, white bass and channel cats from ODNR surveys. Griggs hub: large hybrid striped bass, per ODNR, plus saugeye, crappie, bass, bluegill, white bass and channel cats from ODNR surveys. Indian Lake hub: saugeye, big crappie, bluegill, largemouth, white bass and channel and flathead cats in a shallow lake. Delaware Lake hub: crappie first, plus largemouth and smallmouth, channel cats, bluegill and white bass. What’s biting now

at Hoover

    Changes each month. Typical patterns from the species pages and ODNR, not a fishing report.

    Channel catfish

    Hoover’s workhorse night fish, reliable on cut bait and crawlers from the bank or a 10 hp boat.

    Present at Alum Creek. Night flats and creek channel edges with cut bait or crawlers; not the headline fish here.

    Abundant at Buckeye Lake. Shallow flats near the deeper holes after dark, cut bait or crawlers.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    About half the channel cats in ODNR’s hoop nets were 8 years old, most of them 18 to 20 inches.

    ODNR’s hoop nets caught channel cats at three times the statewide rate, the highest in central Ohio, though most were under 15 inches.

    Blue catfish

    Trophy fishery. Blues were first stocked in 2011 and have become a Hoover highlight.

    Stocked by ODNR at Alum Creek in 2023, 2024 and 2025.

    Stocked by ODNR at O’Shaughnessy in 2022, 2023 and 2025.

    Flathead catfish

    Strong flathead presence. Live or fresh bait near wood, rock, and channel structure after dark.

    A natural flathead population that has grown over the past decade, ODNR says, with 20 to 30 lb fish caught every year.

    Bullhead

    Listed by ODNR at Buckeye Lake. Small, easy night cats on crawlers from the bank; no Ohio daily limit listed.

    Largemouth bass

    More abundant than smallmouth on Hoover. Docks, weeds, and shoreline cover (public water).

    Cove cover and shoreline wood, best in the no wake coves north of Cheshire Road.

    Docks, weeds and shoreline cover in a shallow, fast warming lake.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    Largemouth do well in the shallow water and vegetation, ODNR says, in good numbers though most are under 15 inches.

    A popular bass and tournament lake. ODNR’s largemouth numbers have trended down but are still good, mostly 12 to 15 inches.

    Smallmouth bass

    Present but less abundant than largemouth. Look to rock, points, and clearer main-lake edges.

    A real Alum Creek target. Rock, points and clearer main lake edges, moving up early in spring.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Indian Lake.

    Listed on ODNR’s Delaware State Park page. ODNR hasn’t posted a smallmouth survey for the lake.

    Black crappie

    Solid Hoover panfish: spring banks and brush, then deeper wood and channel edges.

    Coves north of Cheshire Road in spring, then brush, docks and standing wood.

    Buckeye Lake warms first in spring. Shallow docks, stumps and brush, bobbers and minnows.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    ODNR reports lots of black and white crappie over 9 inches, with a real chance at 14 to 16 inch fish.

    Delaware’s best known fish, per ODNR: both crappie species reproduce well and many are over 9 inches.

    White crappie

    Shares Hoover with black crappie, with the same spring bank push and deeper summer brush.

    Shares the coves and wood with black crappie, with the same spring push.

    Same shallow cover as black crappie; spring fish in under 2 ft of water.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    ODNR reports lots of black and white crappie over 9 inches, with a real chance at 14 to 16 inch fish.

    Delaware’s best known fish, per ODNR: both crappie species reproduce well and many are over 9 inches.

    Bluegill / sunfish

    Good bluegill fishing at Hoover: easy bank sessions, and bait for the cooler where legal.

    Easy bank fishing in the coves and around docks at the beach and marina areas (public access only).

    Docks, weeds and shallow bays. Easy summer bank sessions from public shore.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    One of central Ohio’s best bluegill lakes, per ODNR, and one of the few where 8 inch fish come consistently.

    Decent numbers, per ODNR, caught well at different times of the year.

    Yellow perch

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Hoover.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Alum Creek.

    Listed by ODNR at Buckeye Lake. Rock and weed edges, minnows or crawlers near the bottom; less steady than crappie.

    Listed on ODNR’s Indian Lake State Park page among the lake’s plentiful fish. ODNR hasn’t posted a perch survey.

    White bass

    Confirmed on the ODNR Hoover page, with a spring run up Big Walnut Creek and summer schools chasing shad.

    Listed by ODNR for Alum Creek. Schools chasing shad in summer and fall.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Buckeye Lake.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at O’Shaughnessy.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    Abundant, per ODNR. They make a spring spawning run up the South Fork of the Great Miami River near the Moundwood access.

    Decent numbers, per ODNR, caught well at different times of the year.

    Hybrid striped bass (wiper)

    Possible in many Ohio reservoirs and share the striped/white combined limit. White bass are confirmed at Hoover; treat hybrids as “possible / verify reports,” not a guarantee.

    Stocked by ODNR at Buckeye Lake most years, and recorded in its fish surveys.

    Stocked by ODNR at O’Shaughnessy every year from 2019 to 2025, and recorded in its fish surveys.

    ODNR says Griggs has a strong population of large hybrids, often over 20 inches. In summer heat, watch for schools chasing shad and gulls diving.

    Saugeye

    Stocked saugeye have rebounded since about 2017, a real Hoover target for low-light trolling and casting.

    A main Alum Creek target. Dam riprap, causeway rock and channel ledges, best at dusk and after dark.

    Buckeye Lake's signature fish, stocked by ODNR. Riprap, hard bottom and old towpath drop-offs at dusk and night.

    Stocked by ODNR at O’Shaughnessy every year from 2020 to 2025, and recorded in its fish surveys.

    Recorded in ODNR fish surveys at Griggs.

    One of Ohio’s best saugeye lakes, per ODNR: stocked fish survive and grow well in this shallow, productive lake.

    Walleye

    Stocked in 2019 as part of a study against saugeye. They’re here through stocking, so don’t assume a huge wild population.

    Listed for Alum Creek alongside saugeye; they share the combined walleye/saugeye/sauger limit.

    Listed on ODNR’s Indian Lake State Park page. They share the combined walleye, saugeye and sauger limit.

    Muskellunge

    Stocked by ODNR at Alum Creek. A fish of 10,000 casts, best in fall on big baits along weed edges and causeways.

    Common carp

    Incidental and intentional carp fishing on public banks. A good fight, and not on the statewide limits table.

    Longnose gar

    Incidental oddball, occasionally encountered and not a primary Night Bite target.
